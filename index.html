Infrastructure Signal Prospecting Engine

A Python pipeline that turns public network data into a scored, territory-ready prospect list.

77,095 records to 13,857 accounts After scanning about 4,085 data centers and 706 internet exchanges across 28 countries.

Stack: Python, pandas, REST APIs (PeeringDB), Excel output. Designed and directed by me, built with AI coding assistants.

The insight
A company that already runs gear in a data center or peers at an internet exchange has already bought infrastructure. Those companies are the strongest buyers of dedicated internet, wavelengths, cloud connectivity, IP transit, and colocation, but nobody had turned that signal into a list the team could work.
What it does
Pulls two buying signals from PeeringDB, the industry's interconnection directory: data center tenancy and internet exchange membership with port speed. It enriches every record, cleans and deduplicates across both streams so each company appears once, and scores each one 1 to 5 on network type, traffic, ASN presence, and signal type.
How reps use it
Lists split by region, rep, and priority tier, routed down to the city, with technical notes on facilities, exchanges, and ASNs for a more informed first conversation.
Engineering and compliance details

Authenticated API access with rate-limit handling, retries, and logging; website normalization; filtering of education and government entities; transparent rule-based scoring reps can trust. Company-level public data only, with no personal emails, phone numbers, or individual contact data collected.

AI Prospect Intelligence Playbook

An operating manual that turns a general AI assistant into a dedicated prospect analyst.

One input, one decision-ready report Company name, website, or network ASN in; score, product play, buyer roles, and personalized outreach out.

Built with: prompt engineering, AI agents with live web research, structured output design.

The problem
Before a first call, reps pieced together company profile, network footprint, buying signals, and contacts by hand, with no consistent way to judge whether an account deserved their time.
What it does
Researches the account live and returns one standard report: a 100-point score with the breakdown visible so reps can challenge it, a lead product play tied to a specific researched fact, target buyer roles with ready-to-click search links, and a personalized cold email and LinkedIn note. Follow-up commands expand contacts, competitors, signals, or a Salesforce-ready CRM entry.
Why reps trust it
Every data point is tagged verified, reported, or inferred. Every report carries a confidence score. The playbook forbids invented contacts and would rather show a gap than fill it with fiction.

Reps can't work every account. I build the territory and ideal-customer view first, so the team spends time where deals are most likely to close and most valuable when they do.
